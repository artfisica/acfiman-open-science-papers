Open access to scientific resources requires support and organization to become sustained participation. This essay compares CoAfina, the Health for All editathon involving WHO and Cornell, and Venezuelan training experiences using ATLAS Open Data. A documentary synthesis distinguishes products, recognition mechanisms, and pathways for continued engagement. Results include an edition-level CoAfina inventory and a matrix connecting the three formats to preservation, review, and follow-up. The analysis identifies complementary supports: connections between teams and challenges, editorial histories, and academic recognition. We propose organizing each activity around a verifiable contribution, a reusable deliverable, and an assigned follow-up responsibility. The framework offers decisions that Venezuelan institutions can adapt and a basis for collecting comparable evidence on participation and reuse.
