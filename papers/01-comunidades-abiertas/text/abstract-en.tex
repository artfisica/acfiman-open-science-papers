Open access to scientific resources requires organization to sustain participation. This essay compares CoAfina, the Health for All editathon involving WHO and Cornell, and Venezuelan experiences with ATLAS Open Data. A documentary synthesis distinguishes registration, activity, outputs and continuity. We recover public results from three CoAfina editions, the editathon's published definitions and targets, and descriptive ratios with their limitations. The editathons report activity ratios of 42.2\,\% and 26.5\,\%; other formats use different thresholds, preventing comparisons of educational effectiveness. Records identify support associated with submissions, a community channel that persisted, and a transition from participant to challenge proposer. A matrix connects these mechanisms to preservation, recognition and follow-up. We propose reusing operational definitions, linking outputs to their materials and assigning continuing responsibilities. The framework offers decisions adaptable to Venezuelan institutions while distinguishing reach, learning and continuity.
