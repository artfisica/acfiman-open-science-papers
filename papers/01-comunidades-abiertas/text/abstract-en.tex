Access to open resources does not by itself ensure their use or the continuity of a scientific community. This paper synthesizes collaborative experiences connected with Latin America: CoAfina hackathons, the Health for All editathon, and physics training and mentoring activities. Shared tasks, onboarding, mentorship, licensing, and preservation are examined through public documentation and the abstract accepted for ICHEP 2026. The cases illustrate different ways of connecting specialists, students, and digital infrastructure, with indicators that are not directly comparable. An organizational and evaluation framework distinguishes participation, production, reuse, and continuity. The contribution connects previously documented experiences and identifies conditions for adaptation by Venezuelan institutions. No causal effects on learning or retention are asserted without dedicated measurements.
